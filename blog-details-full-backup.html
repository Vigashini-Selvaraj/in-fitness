Why Strength Training Should Be Part of Every Fitness Routine

Alex Mercer Oct 12, 2026 6 min read

When people think of getting in shape, their minds often jump directly to treadmills, stationary bikes, or endless burpees. While cardiovascular conditioning has its indisputable place, there is one foundational pillar of fitness that is repeatedly overlooked by the general population: strength training.

Strength training isn't just about building massive muscles or lifting extremely heavy weights. It's about building a resilient, highly capable machine. It's about longevity, metabolic health, and injury prevention.

"Strength is the glass that holds all other physical qualities. The bigger the glass, the more endurance, mobility, and power you can pour into it."

The Metabolic Advantage

One of the most profound benefits of strength training is its impact on your metabolism. Unlike steady-state cardio, which primarily burns calories while you are actively performing it, lifting weights alters your body composition.

Muscle is metabolically active tissue. This means that for every pound of lean muscle mass you build, your body requires more calories to simply exist. A strength training program effectively increases your resting metabolic rate (RMR), turning your body into an engine that burns energy even when you're sitting on the couch.

Bulletproofing Your Body

As we age, we naturally lose muscle mass and bone density—a condition known as sarcopenia. Resistance training is the ultimate antidote.

  • Bone Density: The stress placed on bones during weightlifting stimulates cells called osteoblasts, which rebuild bone. This helps stave off osteoporosis.
  • Joint Health: Stronger muscles mean better support for your joints. Training the muscles around the knees, hips, and shoulders significantly reduces the risk of everyday injuries.
  • Posture: Modern life has us hunched over screens. Proper strength training corrects these imbalances by strengthening the posterior chain (the muscles on the back of your body).

Mental Resilience

There is a psychological component to lifting weights that cannot be ignored. The process of approaching a heavy bar, doubting if you can lift it, and then successfully completing the rep builds profound mental toughness.

This resilience translates directly into everyday life. When you know you are physically capable of handling heavy resistance, the mental obstacles of a typical workday suddenly seem much more manageable.

How to Get Started

If you've never strength trained before, stepping into the free weight section can be intimidating. Here is the simplest way to start:

Focus on compound movements: the squat, the deadlift, the push-up (or bench press), and the row. These exercises recruit multiple muscle groups at once and give you the best return on your time investment.

More importantly, seek guidance. Form is everything in strength training. At IN+ FITNESS, our coaches are dedicated to ensuring you learn the proper mechanics before we ever worry about how much weight is on the bar.
